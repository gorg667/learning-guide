---
title: Glossary
part: Part V — Putting it together
summary: Definitions of the technical terms used throughout the guide, with pointers to the chapters where each is explained.
---

**Advance organiser** — A brief overview of the structure of material presented before the material itself, giving details something to attach to. ([Ch. 4](04-memory-systems.md), [16](16-reading-to-learn.md))

**Attention residue** — The persistence of attention on a previous task after switching to a new one, degrading performance on the new task. ([Ch. 6](06-attention-focus.md))

**Autonomous stage** — The final stage of skill acquisition in which performance is fast, accurate and largely unconscious; also where improvement stalls unless deliberately reactivated. ([Ch. 18](18-deliberate-practice.md), [33](33-motor-skills-music.md))

**BDNF (brain-derived neurotrophic factor)** — A protein that supports synaptic plasticity and neuron survival; raised by aerobic exercise. ([Ch. 3](03-the-learning-brain.md), [23](23-exercise-nutrition.md))

**Blocked practice** — Practising one topic or skill repeatedly before moving to the next (AAA BBB CCC). Produces good performance during practice and poor retention and discrimination. Contrast *interleaving*. ([Ch. 10](10-interleaving.md))

**Body doubling** — Working in the presence of another person who is also working; improves task initiation and focus, especially with ADHD. ([Ch. 37](37-learning-with-others.md), [38](38-neurodivergence.md))

**Brain dump** — Free recall: writing everything you remember about a topic with the materials closed, then checking. The simplest form of retrieval practice. ([Ch. 8](08-retrieval-practice.md))

**Calibration** — The match between confidence and accuracy in judging one's own knowledge. ([Ch. 13](13-metacognition.md))

**Chunk** — A group of elements bound into a single unit in long-term memory, occupying one slot in working memory. Expertise consists largely of a vast library of chunks. ([Ch. 4](04-memory-systems.md))

**Cognitive load** — The demand placed on working memory during learning; *intrinsic* (inherent to the material), *extraneous* (from poor design or distraction) and *germane* (productive schema-building). ([Ch. 5](05-cognitive-load.md))

**Complementary learning systems** — The theory that the hippocampus learns fast and stores episodes while the neocortex learns slowly and extracts structure, with sleep-dependent transfer between them. ([Ch. 3](03-the-learning-brain.md))

**Concept map** — A network diagram of concepts (nodes) connected by labelled relationships. Contrast *mind map*. ([Ch. 12](12-dual-coding-examples.md))

**Concreteness fading** — Progressing from concrete examples to abstract representations of a concept. ([Ch. 11](11-elaboration-generation.md))

**Consolidation** — The stabilisation and reorganisation of a memory after encoding; *synaptic* (hours) and *systems* (days to years); largely sleep-dependent. ([Ch. 2](02-what-learning-is.md), [22](22-sleep.md))

**Contextual interference** — The motor-learning term for the interleaving effect: random practice order impairs acquisition and improves retention and transfer. ([Ch. 33](33-motor-skills-music.md))

**Cornell method** — A note-taking layout with a cue column, a notes column and a summary, designed to convert notes into retrieval prompts. ([Ch. 15](15-note-taking.md))

**Deliberate practice** — Effortful, goal-directed practice at the edge of ability, targeting weaknesses, with immediate feedback and repetition with refinement. Contrast *naive practice*. ([Ch. 18](18-deliberate-practice.md))

**Desirable difficulty** — A condition that makes learning harder in the moment but improves long-term retention and transfer: spacing, interleaving, retrieval, generation, variation. ([Ch. 7](07-desirable-difficulties.md))

**Dual coding** — Representing information in both verbal and visual form, producing two memory traces and using both channels of working memory. Not the same as "learning styles". ([Ch. 12](12-dual-coding-examples.md))

**Effect size (d, g)** — A standardised measure of the size of a difference between groups, in standard deviations. Roughly: 0.2 small, 0.5 medium, 0.8 large. ([Ch. 1](01-introduction.md))

**Elaboration** — Adding to, connecting and explaining new information in terms of existing knowledge. ([Ch. 11](11-elaboration-generation.md))

**Elaborative interrogation** — Asking "why is this true?" of a fact and answering from one's own knowledge. ([Ch. 11](11-elaboration-generation.md))

**Element interactivity** — The number of elements that must be held in working memory simultaneously to understand something; the main determinant of intrinsic load. ([Ch. 5](05-cognitive-load.md))

**Encoding** — The initial registration of an experience into memory. ([Ch. 2](02-what-learning-is.md))

**Encoding specificity** — The principle that a retrieval cue is effective to the extent it was present at encoding. ([Ch. 4](04-memory-systems.md))

**Episodic memory** — Memory for personally experienced events, embedded in time and place. ([Ch. 2](02-what-learning-is.md))

**Expertise reversal effect** — Instructional techniques that help novices (worked examples, heavy guidance) become unhelpful or harmful for more expert learners. ([Ch. 5](05-cognitive-load.md))

**Expressive writing** — Writing about one's worries for about ten minutes before a stressful task; reduces the working-memory cost of anxiety. ([Ch. 24](24-stress-emotion.md))

**External focus** — Attending to the effect of a movement (the target, the sound) rather than the body; improves motor learning. ([Ch. 33](33-motor-skills-music.md))

**Fading** — Gradually removing scaffolding — for example, worked examples with progressively more steps left for the learner. ([Ch. 5](05-cognitive-load.md), [12](12-dual-coding-examples.md))

**Feynman technique** — Explaining a concept in plain language from memory, identifying gaps, and refining. ([Ch. 20](20-problem-solving-teaching.md))

**Flow** — A state of absorbed, effortless concentration with merged action and awareness, produced by clear goals, immediate feedback and matched challenge and skill. ([Ch. 29](29-flow-difficulty.md))

**Fluency illusion** — Mistaking ease of processing (from recency, familiarity, the presence of the answer) for knowledge. ([Ch. 7](07-desirable-difficulties.md))

**Forgetting curve** — Ebbinghaus's finding that retention drops rapidly at first and then more slowly; each successful relearning flattens it. ([Ch. 2](02-what-learning-is.md))

**FSRS** — Free Spaced Repetition Scheduler; a machine-learning scheduling algorithm modelling difficulty, stability and retrievability; built into Anki. ([Ch. 9](09-spaced-repetition.md))

**Generation effect** — Information produced by the learner is remembered better than information received. ([Ch. 11](11-elaboration-generation.md))

**Growth mindset** — The belief that abilities can be developed through effort and strategy. Interventions have small average effects, concentrated in struggling students. ([Ch. 26](26-mindset-beliefs.md))

**Guidance hypothesis** — In motor learning, feedback after every attempt aids performance but can impair retention by preventing self-evaluation. ([Ch. 33](33-motor-skills-music.md))

**Habit stacking** — Attaching a new behaviour to an existing routine that serves as its cue. ([Ch. 27](27-habits-environment.md))

**Hippocampus** — The medial temporal lobe structure essential for forming new declarative memories and for spatial memory. ([Ch. 3](03-the-learning-brain.md))

**Hypercorrection effect** — Errors made with high confidence are better remembered after correction than low-confidence errors. ([Ch. 7](07-desirable-difficulties.md))

**Implementation intention** — A plan of the form "When X, I will do Y", which roughly doubles follow-through on effortful goals. ([Ch. 25](25-motivation.md), [27](27-habits-environment.md))

**Incubation** — Improvement in problem solving after a break from the problem. ([Ch. 20](20-problem-solving-teaching.md))

**Inert knowledge** — Knowledge that is possessed but not applied outside the context in which it was learned. ([Ch. 19](19-transfer-mental-models.md))

**Interference** — Forgetting caused by competition between memories; *retroactive* (new disrupts old) and *proactive* (old disrupts new). ([Ch. 4](04-memory-systems.md))

**Interleaving** — Mixing different topics or problem types within a practice session (ABCBCA). Improves discrimination, retention and transfer. ([Ch. 10](10-interleaving.md))

**Intrinsic / extrinsic motivation** — Doing something for its own sake versus for a separable outcome. ([Ch. 25](25-motivation.md))

**Judgment of learning (JOL)** — A learner's prediction of future recall; accurate when delayed and retrieval-based, inflated when immediate and fluency-based. ([Ch. 13](13-metacognition.md))

**Keyword method** — A vocabulary mnemonic linking a foreign word to a similar-sounding known word through an image. ([Ch. 17](17-mnemonics.md))

**Lag effect** — Longer gaps between study sessions generally produce better long-term retention, up to a point dependent on the retention interval. ([Ch. 9](09-spaced-repetition.md))

**Learning vs performance** — The distinction between relatively permanent change (learning) and what one can do right now (performance); the two can move in opposite directions. ([Ch. 2](02-what-learning-is.md), [7](07-desirable-difficulties.md))

**Learning styles** — The (unsupported) claim that matching instruction to a learner's preferred modality improves learning. ([Ch. 14](14-what-doesnt-work.md))

**Long-term potentiation (LTP)** — The strengthening of a synapse by repeated activation; the cellular basis of learning. ([Ch. 3](03-the-learning-brain.md))

**Major system** — A mnemonic code mapping digits to consonant sounds so numbers can be turned into words. ([Ch. 17](17-mnemonics.md))

**Mastery goal / performance goal** — Aiming to understand and improve versus aiming to demonstrate ability relative to others. ([Ch. 25](25-motivation.md))

**Mental model** — A general explanatory pattern or principle applicable across domains. ([Ch. 19](19-transfer-mental-models.md))

**Metacognition** — Knowledge of and control over one's own cognitive processes; monitoring and regulation of learning. ([Ch. 13](13-metacognition.md))

**Method of loci (memory palace)** — A mnemonic placing vivid images of items at locations along a familiar route. ([Ch. 17](17-mnemonics.md))

**Mind map** — A radial diagram of topics and subtopics around a central idea. Less demanding than a concept map. ([Ch. 12](12-dual-coding-examples.md))

**Myelin** — The insulating sheath around axons that speeds conduction; increases with use of a pathway. ([Ch. 3](03-the-learning-brain.md))

**Neuroplasticity** — The brain's capacity to change its structure and connections in response to experience, throughout life. ([Ch. 3](03-the-learning-brain.md))

**Notional machine** — A learner's mental model of what the computer does when a program runs. ([Ch. 32](32-programming.md))

**OK plateau** — The level at which a skill becomes automatic and "good enough" and stops improving. ([Ch. 18](18-deliberate-practice.md))

**Parsons problem** — A programming exercise in which scrambled lines of a correct program are reassembled. ([Ch. 32](32-programming.md))

**Peer instruction** — A teaching method in which students answer a conceptual question, discuss with peers, and answer again. ([Ch. 37](37-learning-with-others.md))

**Pomodoro technique** — Working in timed blocks (classically 25 minutes) with short breaks. ([Ch. 28](28-time-management.md))

**Pretesting / prequestion effect** — Attempting questions before studying improves learning of the material, even when the attempts fail. ([Ch. 7](07-desirable-difficulties.md), [8](08-retrieval-practice.md))

**Procedural memory** — Memory for skills and habits; learned slowly through practice, largely unconscious, highly durable. ([Ch. 2](02-what-learning-is.md))

**Productive failure** — Attempting to solve problems before receiving instruction, which improves conceptual understanding and transfer from the subsequent instruction. ([Ch. 5](05-cognitive-load.md), [7](07-desirable-difficulties.md))

**Protégé effect** — Learning more when learning in order to teach. ([Ch. 20](20-problem-solving-teaching.md))

**Redundancy effect** — Presenting the same information simultaneously in two forms (e.g., text read aloud) impairs learning. ([Ch. 5](05-cognitive-load.md))

**Retrieval practice (testing effect)** — Recalling information from memory strengthens it more than restudying it. ([Ch. 8](08-retrieval-practice.md))

**Retrieval strength / storage strength** — How accessible a memory is right now versus how well learned it is; gains in storage strength are greatest when retrieval strength is low. ([Ch. 4](04-memory-systems.md))

**Schema** — An organised knowledge structure that makes new information meaningful and can be handled as a single unit in working memory. ([Ch. 4](04-memory-systems.md))

**Self-determination theory** — The theory that motivation depends on satisfaction of three needs: autonomy, competence and relatedness. ([Ch. 25](25-motivation.md))

**Self-efficacy** — Belief in one's ability to succeed at a specific task; a strong predictor of achievement, built mainly by mastery experiences. ([Ch. 26](26-mindset-beliefs.md))

**Self-explanation** — Explaining to oneself the meaning and rationale of material or of steps in a worked example. ([Ch. 11](11-elaboration-generation.md))

**Semantic memory** — General knowledge stripped of the context in which it was learned. ([Ch. 2](02-what-learning-is.md))

**Slow-wave sleep** — Deep NREM sleep, concentrated early in the night, most important for consolidating declarative memory. ([Ch. 22](22-sleep.md))

**Spacing effect** — Study distributed over time produces better retention than the same amount massed. ([Ch. 9](09-spaced-repetition.md))

**Split-attention effect** — Separating information that must be integrated (a diagram and its explanation) impairs learning. ([Ch. 5](05-cognitive-load.md))

**SQ3R** — Survey, Question, Read, Recite, Review: a structured active-reading method. ([Ch. 16](16-reading-to-learn.md))

**Successive relearning** — Retrieval practice to a criterion, repeated in several spaced sessions. ([Ch. 8](08-retrieval-practice.md), [9](09-spaced-repetition.md))

**Targeted memory reactivation (TMR)** — Presenting cues during sleep that were associated with learning, to strengthen those memories. ([Ch. 22](22-sleep.md))

**Transfer** — Applying knowledge or skill learned in one context to a different one; *near* (similar contexts) or *far* (dissimilar). ([Ch. 19](19-transfer-mental-models.md))

**Transient information effect** — Spoken or animated information, once presented, is gone; long segments overload working memory. ([Ch. 5](05-cognitive-load.md))

**Undermining effect** — Extrinsic rewards for an intrinsically motivated activity can reduce intrinsic motivation. ([Ch. 25](25-motivation.md))

**Variable practice** — Practising a skill across a range of conditions to build generalisable knowledge. ([Ch. 10](10-interleaving.md), [33](33-motor-skills-music.md))

**Worked example** — A problem presented with its full step-by-step solution; more effective than problem solving for novices when self-explained. ([Ch. 5](05-cognitive-load.md), [12](12-dual-coding-examples.md))

**Working memory** — The limited-capacity system (about four chunks, a few seconds) in which conscious thinking occurs. ([Ch. 4](04-memory-systems.md))

**Zettelkasten** — A note-taking system of atomic, linked, permanent notes in one's own words. ([Ch. 15](15-note-taking.md))

**Zone of proximal development** — The range of tasks a learner can do with support but not yet alone; where learning happens. ([Ch. 29](29-flow-difficulty.md))
